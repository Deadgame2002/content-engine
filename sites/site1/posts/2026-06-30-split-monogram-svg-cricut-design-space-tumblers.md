Split monograms are huge right now. You see them everywhere. On tumblers, shirts, and home decor. They look fancy but are surprisingly easy to make.

This guide walks you through every step. You will learn how to make a split monogram SVG right in Cricut Design Space. No extra software needed. Just your Cricut and a little patience.

## What Is a Split Monogram SVG?

A split monogram is a design where the middle letter is larger. The left and right letters sit smaller beside it. They often touch or overlap slightly. This creates a beautiful, custom look.

For tumblers, split monograms are perfect. They fit well on curved surfaces. They look elegant in vinyl. And they make amazing personalized gifts.

Unlike a standard monogram, the split version has a gap or a decorative line between the letters. This "split" gives it a modern, airy feel. Think of it as the little black dress of monograms—classy but trendy.

## Best Fonts for a Split Monogram on Tumblers

Not every font works for split monograms. You need something with a bit of flair. Script fonts are popular. But thin scripts can be hard to weed. Thick, bold scripts work better.

Here are my go-to font styles:

- **Thick script fonts** – Easy to cut and weed. Great for beginners.
- **Swirly serif fonts** – Elegant and classic. Perfect for wedding gifts.
- **Modern calligraphy fonts** – Trendy and readable. Ideal for young recipients.
- **Cursive fonts with bouncy letters** – Fun and playful. Good for kids' tumblers.

Avoid fonts with very thin strokes. They can tear during weeding. Also skip fonts with too many decorative loops. They can look messy when welded.

> **Pro tip:** Use a font that has both uppercase and lowercase versions for the same letter. Some monograms look better with a lowercase 'a' for the side letters.

## Step 1: Open Cricut Design Space and Set Up Your Canvas

Open Cricut Design Space. Start a new project. Your canvas will appear. It looks empty now. But soon it will hold your beautiful monogram.

Click on the canvas. A small toolbar appears at the top. Find the "Text" button. It looks like a 'T'. Click it. A text box appears on your canvas.

Type three letters. For example, "J A M" for a name. Leave a space between each letter. This makes them easier to position later.

## Step 2: Choose and Size Your Font

With the text box selected, look at the top panel. You see a font dropdown. Click it. Search for your chosen font. I recommend using a thick script font for tumblers.

If you have a font from Creative Fabrica installed, it will appear here. Otherwise, use one of Cricut's free fonts. "Cricut Sans" is not great for monograms. But "Megan" or "Becky" work well.

Now resize the middle letter. Click on the text box. Double-click inside the text. Highlight the middle letter. Change its font size to about 200. Leave the side letters at 100. Adjust until the middle letter is noticeably larger.

## Step 3: Position the Letters Correctly

Split monograms look best when the letters are close. But not touching yet. Move each letter individually.

Click on the middle letter. Drag it down slightly. It should sit lower than the side letters. For script fonts, the middle letter often has a long tail or swash. Position the side letters to just touch that swash.

For serif or sans-serif fonts, align the middle letter's bottom with the side letters' center. This creates a balanced look.

## Step 4: Weld the Letters Together

Welding joins the letters into one single shape. This is crucial for vinyl cutting. Without welding, each letter cuts separately. That can cause misalignment.

Select all three letters. Hold Shift and click each one. Or drag a selection box around them. Then click the "Weld" button in the bottom right panel. It looks like two overlapping circles.

After welding, the letters become one shape. You can move and resize them as a single piece.

## Step 5: Add a Decorative Split Line (Optional but Beautiful)

Many split monograms have a decorative line between the letters. This line can be a simple straight line or a curvy swash.

![Cricut cutting machine mid-cut with split monogram vinyl letters on mat](https://i.ibb.co/hFpVmyWN/site1-split-monogram-svg-cricut-design-space-tumblers-body.jpg)

Click the "Shapes" button on the left panel. Choose a line or a curve. Place it between the left and middle letters. Adjust its thickness. Use the line settings in the top panel.

Repeat for the right side. Make sure the lines are parallel and evenly spaced. This adds a professional touch.

If you want a fancier look, use a decorative SVG element. A small heart or star works well for a personal touch.

## Step 6: Duplicate and Offset for Layered Vinyl

Layered vinyl looks amazing on tumblers. But it requires multiple cuts. You need a separate layer for each color.

First, duplicate your welded monogram. Select it and press Ctrl+C then Ctrl+V. Now you have two copies.

Select one copy. Click the "Offset" button in the top panel. Choose a small offset—about 0.1 inch. This creates a border around the monogram. The border will be your bottom layer. The original monogram will be your top layer.

## Step 7: Prepare the File for Cutting

Now you have two layers. The original monogram and its offset border. Each layer should be a different color. This helps you keep track during weeding.

Select the offset layer. Change its color in the top panel. Choose a contrasting color, like black or dark gray. The original monogram can stay white or light gray.

Group each layer separately. Select the offset and click the "Group" button. Do the same for the original monogram. This keeps your file organized.

## Step 8: Cut and Weed the Vinyl

Load your cutting mat with vinyl. Place the vinyl shiny side up. Select your material in Design Space. For most permanent vinyl, choose "Permanent Vinyl".

Click "Make It". Your two layers will appear on separate mats. Cut each layer separately. Use a weeding tool to remove excess vinyl from the offset layer first. Then weed the top layer.

Transfer tape is your friend. Apply it to the weeded offset layer. Press firmly. Then carefully peel the backing. Place it on your tumbler. Use a scraper tool to remove bubbles.

Peel away the transfer tape. Now apply the top layer the same way. Line it up exactly with the offset. This creates a beautiful layered effect.

## Step 9: Seal and Finish

Tumblers get handled a lot. Vinyl can peel over time. A sealant helps protect your work.

Use a clear acrylic spray or Mod Podge. Apply a thin, even coat. Let it dry completely. Then apply a second coat. Your monogram will last much longer.

For dishwasher-safe tumblers, use epoxy resin. But that requires more equipment. Spray sealant works well for most projects.

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Even experienced crafters mess up. Here are the most common split monogram mistakes:

- **Letters too far apart** – Split monograms need tight spacing. Use the arrow keys to nudge letters closer.
- **Font too thin** – Thin fonts are hard to weed. Stick to bold or medium weights.
- **No offset layer** – Layering without an offset looks sloppy. Always add a small border.
- **Welding before positioning** – Once welded, letters are stuck. Position them perfectly first.

## Часто задаваемые вопросы

### Can I make a split monogram SVG without welding?

Technically yes, but welding is highly recommended. Without welding, each letter cuts separately and may shift during transfer. Welding keeps everything aligned.

### What font size should I use for a split monogram on a 20 oz tumbler?

For the middle letter, start at 2.5 inches tall. Side letters should be about 1.5 inches. Adjust based on your font's thickness and the tumbler's curve.

### Do I need a special font for split monograms?

Not necessarily. Any script or serif font can work. However, fonts with thick strokes and clear letter shapes are easier to weed and look better on vinyl.

### How do I add a split line between the letters?

Use the Shapes tool in Cricut Design Space to add a line or curve. Place it between the left and middle letters, then duplicate it for the right side. Adjust thickness and color.

### Can I use this method for other projects like shirts or signs?

Absolutely. The same split monogram technique works for T-shirts, wooden signs, mugs, and more. Just adjust the material settings and vinyl type accordingly.
