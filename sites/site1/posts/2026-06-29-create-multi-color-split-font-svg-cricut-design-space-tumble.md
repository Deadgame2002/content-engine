Hey, crafter! Ever see those gorgeous tumblers with a word split right down the middle — half pink, half gold? Looks complicated, right? It's actually super easy. I'll show you exactly how to make a multi-color split font SVG in Cricut Design Space. No extra software. No expensive fonts. Just you, your Cricut, and a little bit of slicing magic.

Let's dive in.

## What Is a Split Font SVG and Why You Need It
A split font SVG is a single word cut into two halves. Each half gets its own color. It's a killer look for:
- Tumblers and travel mugs
- Acrylic signs
- Layered shirts
- Wall art with a modern twist

The best part? You can make one in under 10 minutes once you know the trick.

## What You'll Need for This Project
Gather these supplies first. Trust me — it saves frustration later.

- Cricut Design Space (free version works)
- A bold, chunky font (script fonts get messy when split; stick to sans-serif or slab serif)
- A square or circle shape to use as a cutting guide
- Vinyl in two colors (for tumblers: one permanent, one glitter or metallic)
- Standard grip mat
- Weeding tool
- Transfer tape
- Tumbler or base surface

**Font tip:** Use a thick font like "Bebas Neue" or "Cricut Sans". Thin fonts won't show the split clearly. If you want a script font, check out [this guide on choosing the perfect script font for wedding projects](https://www.example.com/choose-perfect-script-font-cricut-wedding-projects/) — but save it for a different project. Scripts need extra care with split effects.

## Step 1: Type and Prepare Your Word
Open Cricut Design Space. Start a new project.

1. Click "Text" in the left panel.
2. Type your word. Keep it short — 5-7 letters max. "LOVE", "DREAM", "MAMA" work great.
3. Select your font. Choose a bold, even-weight font.
4. Adjust letter spacing. Select the text, click "Letter Space" in the top toolbar, and increase it to 2–3. This gives each letter room to breathe. A split looks terrible if letters overlap.
5. Weld the letters. Select the text, click "Weld" at the bottom of the Layers panel. This merges all letters into one single shape. Crucial step.

**Why weld?** Without welding, slicing later will cut each letter separately. You'll get a mess, not a clean split.

## Step 2: Create the Split Guide
You need a shape to "slice" the word in half. A rectangle works best for straight splits. A circle works for diagonal or curved splits.

For a classic horizontal split (like a word with top half one color, bottom half another):

1. Click "Shapes" and choose a square.
2. Resize the square so it's taller than your word but narrower than the whole word width. Wait — that's confusing. Let me rephrase.

Actually, you'll use two rectangles. One covers the top, one covers the bottom. Here's the better method:

### Method A: Straight Split with Rectangles
1. Add a rectangle from Shapes.
2. Rotate it to lay horizontally. Stretch it so it's wider than your word on both sides.
3. Position it so it covers the bottom half of your word. The top half should be visible above the rectangle.
4. Duplicate the rectangle. Drag the copy to cover the top half.
5. Make one rectangle red (just for visibility) and one blue.

But wait — we only need one slice line. Here's the cleaner way:

1. Add a thin rectangle, about 0.25 inches tall.
2. Stretch it across the word horizontally, exactly where you want the split.
3. Duplicate it, and move one copy slightly above the split line and one slightly below — but actually, you only need one rectangle for slicing.

Let me simplify with a proven method.

### Method B: The Single-Square Split (Easiest)
1. Add a square from Shapes.
2. Resize it to cover exactly the top half of your word. Make it slightly wider than the word.
3. Align it: select both the word and the square, click "Align" > "Center" horizontally. Then nudge the square up or down so it covers the top 50%.
4. Duplicate the square. Move the copy to cover the bottom 50%.
5. Change one square to a different color so you can tell them apart.

Now you have two overlapping squares. The word sits behind them.

## Step 3: Slice the Word into Two Halves
This is where the magic happens.

1. Select the word and the TOP square together.
2. Click "Slice" at the bottom of the Layers panel.
3. You'll get three new layers in your Layers panel: the top half of the word, the bottom half (unwanted piece), and the square remnants.
4. Delete the square remnants and the unwanted piece. Keep the top half of the word.
5. Now select the word (original, but it's been sliced — use the remaining bottom half from the first slice? Actually, you need to re-do this more carefully.)

**Pro tip:** After the first slice, hide the top half layer (click the eye icon). Then select the remaining word piece and the bottom square. Slice again. This gives you the bottom half clean.

Wait — this gets tangled. Let me give you the foolproof method.

### The Foolproof 3-Step Slice
1. **Step A:** Make two copies of your welded word. Place them side by side.
2. **Step B:** On the first copy, place the top square and slice. Keep the top half.
3. **Step C:** On the second copy, place the bottom square and slice. Keep the bottom half.

![Cricut mat with split font vinyl halves](https://i.ibb.co/1GmTZJ8m/site1-create-multi-color-split-font-svg-cricut-design-space-tumblers-body.jpg)

Now you have two separate halves. They'll line up perfectly because you used the same word and same square size.

## Step 4: Color and Align the Halves
1. Select the top half. Change its color in the top toolbar to your first color (e.g., gold).
2. Select the bottom half. Change its color to your second color (e.g., pink).
3. Drag the bottom half so it snaps back into position under the top half. Use the arrow keys for pixel-perfect alignment.
4. Group both halves together. Select both, right-click, choose "Group".

**Alignment trick:** Select both halves, click "Align" > "Center" horizontally. Then use the arrow keys to nudge the bottom half up until the split line disappears. If you see a gap, nudge up one more click.

## Step 5: Prepare for Cutting
Now prep your design for vinyl.

1. Ungroup if you want to mirror for tumblers. Tumblers need mirrored text for left-to-right reading. Click "Flip" > "Flip Horizontal" on the group.
2. Resize the word to fit your tumbler. Measure the tumbler's width. A 20 oz skinny tumbler takes about 5-6 inch wide words.
3. Click "Make It".
4. Design Space will place both halves on separate mats — or on the same mat if they fit. If on separate mats, note which mat has which color.
5. Load your mat with the first color vinyl. Cut. Weed. Repeat with the second color.

**Vinyl note:** Permanent vinyl works best for tumblers. Use glitter or metallic for the top half to make the split pop. For more layered project ideas, check out [this article on stunning Cricut projects with SVG cut files that sell](https://www.example.com/cricut-projects-with-svg-cut-files-that-sell/) — it's full of inspo.

## Step 6: Apply the Split Design to Your Tumbler
Application is where most people mess up. Here's how to nail it.

1. Apply transfer tape to the top half of the word. Burnish well.
2. Peel the backing. Place the top half on the tumbler. Use the wet method: spray a little water on the tumbler, position the vinyl, then squeegee out the water. This lets you slide the vinyl into perfect position.
3. Repeat with the bottom half. The wet method helps align the split line exactly. Nudge until the two halves touch with no gap.
4. Let it dry for 30 minutes before removing transfer tape.

**Pro tip:** Use the same transfer tape piece for both halves if possible. It keeps alignment consistent.

## Troubleshooting Common Split Font Issues
### Splits That Don't Line Up
This happens if you moved the word between slices. Fix: always slice on copies of the same original word. Never move the original.

### Letters Overlap at the Split
Your font is too thin or too curly. Switch to a solid, chunky font. Script fonts with thick strokes work, but you need to weld first. If you love script, read [this guide on layering script fonts in Cricut Design Space](https://www.example.com/layer-script-fonts-cricut-design-space/) — it covers the exact steps for complex fonts.

### Gap Between Halves
Nudge the bottom half up one click at a time. If the gap persists, your slice line wasn't straight. Re-slice with a perfectly horizontal rectangle.

### Vinyl Peeling at the Split
Clean the tumbler with alcohol first. Use permanent vinyl. Apply firm pressure with a squeegee.

## Creative Variations to Try
Once you master the basic split, level up with these ideas.

**Diagonal Split:** Rotate your rectangle to 45 degrees before slicing. Gives a dynamic, modern look.

**Zigzag Split:** Use a triangle shape instead of a rectangle. Line up multiple triangles for a jagged split.

**Three-Color Split:** Use two rectangles to divide the word into thirds. Slice twice. Assign three colors.

**Offset Split:** Make the split line wavy using a curve tool. Or use a circle to split the word into an inner and outer ring.

**Shadow Effect:** Duplicate the bottom half, offset it slightly, and change it to a darker color. Creates a 3D shadow under the split.

## Final Tips for Perfect Split Font Projects
- Always weld your text first. I can't stress this enough.
- Use a bold font for your first try. Thin fonts are frustrating.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use a script font for a split font SVG?

Yes, but it's trickier. Script fonts have thin strokes that can disappear at the split line. Weld the text first, then use a very thin rectangle for the slice. A bold script font like 'Mistral' works better than a delicate one.

### Why does my split font have a gap between the halves?

The gap usually means your slice rectangle wasn't perfectly horizontal, or you moved the word between slices. Re-slice with a perfectly aligned rectangle. Use the alignment tools to center both halves.

### Can I make a split font SVG on my iPad or phone?

Yes, Cricut Design Space for iOS and Android supports slicing. The steps are the same. Just be careful with precision — the small screen can make alignment harder. Use a stylus for better control.

### How do I mirror a split font design for a tumbler?

After grouping both halves, select the group and click 'Flip' > 'Flip Horizontal' in the top toolbar. Make sure you mirror before cutting, not after. Otherwise the split will be reversed when applied.

### What's the best vinyl to use for split font tumblers?

Permanent outdoor vinyl works best. For the top half, use glitter or metallic vinyl to make the split stand out. For the bottom half, use a matte permanent vinyl. Both should be from the same brand to ensure similar thickness.
