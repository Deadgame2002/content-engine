There is something almost magical about the way calligraphy fonts dance across a wedding invitation—the elegant swirls, the delicate flourishes, the way a single word can feel like a whispered promise. Yet for many DIY brides, that magic can quickly turn into frustration when the font doesn't behave as expected on screen or on paper. I have seen countless beautifully designed invitations ruined by subtle errors that could have been avoided with a little know-how. In this article, I will walk you through the five most critical mistakes I have encountered when working with wedding calligraphy fonts, and more importantly, I will show you exactly how to fix each one so your invitations look as polished as a professional calligrapher's work.

## 1. Ignoring Letter Spacing (Tracking) in Calligraphy Fonts

One of the most overlooked details in DIY wedding invitations is the spacing between letters. Calligraphy fonts are designed to flow naturally, but when you type them in a standard design program like Cricut Design Space or Adobe Illustrator, the default tracking can be either too tight or too loose. The result is a cluttered, hard-to-read word that loses all its elegance.

### Why Default Tracking Fails for Calligraphy

Most calligraphy fonts are created with a specific rhythm in mind. The ascenders and descenders are meant to overlap slightly, creating a cohesive script. However, when you paste text into a design canvas, the software often applies a uniform letter spacing that ignores these nuances. For example, the letter combination "th" might need extra space, while "ll" might look awkward with too much gap.

### How to Fix Spacing in Cricut Design Space

To adjust tracking properly, follow these steps:

- **Step 1:** Select your text layer in Cricut Design Space.
- **Step 2:** Click on the "Letter Space" icon (it looks like two As with arrows between them).
- **Step 3:** Decrease the value if letters are too spread out, or increase it if they are crammed together. A good starting point is -1 to -3 for most calligraphy fonts.
- **Step 4:** Zoom in and manually check each letter pair. Pay special attention to combinations like "fi", "fl", and "tt".

### Manual Kerning for Precision

If the font still looks off, consider using the "Ungroup to Lines" feature in Design Space to separate each letter, then manually reposition them using the arrow keys. This is time-consuming but gives you complete control. ## 2. Choosing the Wrong Font Size for Your Invitation Dimensions

A calligraphy font that looks breathtaking at 72 points on your monitor can appear tiny and illegible when printed at 5x7 inches. Conversely, a font set at 120 points might overwhelm the entire card. This is a surprisingly common mistake that can throw off the entire composition of your invitation.

### Matching Font Size to Card Size

Here is a quick reference table for standard invitation sizes:

| Card Size | Recommended Font Size (Main Text) | Recommended Font Size (Names) |
|-----------|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| 5x7 inches | 48-60 pt | 36-48 pt |
| A2 (4.25x5.5 inches) | 36-48 pt | 28-36 pt |
| A7 (5x7 inches with envelope) | 44-56 pt | 32-44 pt |
| 4x6 inches | 32-44 pt | 24-32 pt |

### Testing Before Cutting

Always print a test on plain cardstock before cutting your final sheets. Place the printed test inside an actual envelope to see how the text scales in context. I recommend using a lightbox or holding the card at arm's length to simulate how guests will view it. If the text feels too small, increase by 6-8 points and test again.

## 3. Forgetting to Weld Calligraphy Fonts for Cutting

This mistake is particularly common among those new to Cricut or Silhouette machines. When you type a word in a calligraphy font, the software sees each letter as a separate object. If you cut without welding, you will end up with individual letters that need to be painstakingly glued into place—a nightmare for any DIY bride.

### What Welding Does

Welding combines overlapping letter paths into a single continuous shape. This is essential for script fonts because the letters are meant to connect. Without welding, the machine cuts around each letter individually, leaving gaps where the strokes should join.

### How to Weld in Cricut Design Space

- **Step 1:** Select all the text layers you want to weld.
- **Step 2:** Click the "Weld" button in the bottom-right corner of the Layers panel.
- **Step 3:** Check the preview to ensure the letters are connected properly. If they are not, adjust letter spacing (as discussed in mistake #1) and try again.
- **Step 4:** Once welded, you can resize the entire word without breaking the connections.

### Welding vs. Attaching

Do not confuse welding with attaching. Attaching simply locks the relative positions of layers but does not merge their paths. For calligraphy fonts, always use weld for the main text. For decorative elements like SVGs, you might use attach to keep them in place. ## 4. Overlooking Glyphs and Alternate Characters

Many calligraphy fonts come with a treasure trove of alternate characters—swashes, ligatures, and stylistic sets—that can transform an ordinary word into a work of art. Yet most DIY brides never even open the glyph panel, settling for the default letters that may lack the flourish their design deserves.

### Accessing Glyphs in Design Software

In Cricut Design Space, you cannot directly access glyphs, but you can use the Character Map UWP tool on Windows or the Font Book app on Mac to copy and paste special characters. Alternatively, use Adobe Illustrator or InDesign for full glyph support.

### When to Use Swashes

Swashes are best used sparingly. A single decorative stroke at the beginning of the bride's name or at the end of the date can add elegance. Overusing them, however, can make the text look messy. For example, use a swash on the first letter of the couple's names but keep the rest simple.

### How to Insert Glyphs

- **Step 1:** Open the Character Map or Font Book and select your calligraphy font.
- **Step 2:** Browse through the glyphs and click on the one you want.
- **Step 3:** Copy the glyph (Ctrl+C or Cmd+C).
- **Step 4:** Paste it into your design software and adjust the size and position as needed.

Some fonts even have multiple swash variations for the same letter. Experiment with different versions to see which fits your invitation's aesthetic best.

## 5. Relying Only on One Style of Calligraphy Font

A common trap is falling in love with a single calligraphy font and using it for everything: the main text, the names, the date, and even the envelope addressing. While consistency is important, using just one font can make your invitation feel flat and monotonous.

### Building a Font Hierarchy

A well-designed invitation uses a hierarchy of fonts: a primary script font for the couple's names, a secondary script or sans-serif for the event details, and a simple serif for the addresses or secondary information. This creates visual interest and guides the reader's eye through the content.

Here is an example of a successful font pairing:

- **Names:** A bold, flowing calligraphy font like "Mistral" or "Great Vibes".
- **Event Details:** A clean, readable script like "Alex Brush" or "Dancing Script".
- **Date and Location:** A modern serif like "Playfair Display" or a minimalist sans-serif like "Montserrat".

### How to Select Complementary Fonts

- **Contrast:** Pair a highly decorative script with a simple sans-serif to avoid competition.
- **Mood:** Match the font style to your wedding theme. Rustic weddings pair well with hand-drawn scripts, while modern weddings benefit from clean, geometric fonts.
- **Legibility:** Always test the secondary fonts at small sizes. A beautiful script that is unreadable at 12 points is useless for address lines.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does my calligraphy font look broken when I cut it on Cricut?

This usually happens because you forgot to weld the text. Welding merges overlapping letter paths into a single shape, preventing the machine from cutting each letter separately. Always weld calligraphy fonts before cutting.

### How do I fix uneven spacing between letters in a calligraphy font?

Adjust the letter spacing (tracking) in your design software. In Cricut Design Space, use the Letter Space tool to decrease or increase the gap. For precise control, ungroup the letters and manually reposition them using the arrow keys.

### Can I use calligraphy fonts for envelope addressing?

Yes, but choose fonts with high legibility at small sizes. Script fonts with open counters and clear strokes work best. Avoid overly ornate fonts for addresses, as they can be hard to read.

### What is the best font size for a 5x7 invitation card?

For the main text (like the couple's names), use 48-60 points. For event details, use 36-48 points. Always print a test on plain cardstock to see how the size looks in real life before cutting.

### How do I add swashes to letters in Cricut Design Space?

Cricut Design Space does not natively support glyphs, so use Character Map (Windows) or Font Book (Mac) to copy special characters. Paste them into your design and adjust the size and position as needed.
