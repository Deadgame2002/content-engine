Choosing the right wedding invitation cardstock is the single most important decision you'll make for your DIY invitations — it determines the feel, the print quality, and the overall luxury factor. With so many options, it's easy to feel overwhelmed, but this guide breaks down the top 7 cardstock types for 2025, complete with weights, textures, and printing tips.

## Key Takeaways
- Cardstock weight matters: 110 lb (300 gsm) is the sweet spot for most invitation suites.
- Cotton paper offers a soft, elegant texture that photographs beautifully.
- Pearl shimmer adds a subtle iridescence perfect for evening weddings.
- Matte finishes reduce glare, making them ideal for calligraphy and foil.
- Always test your printer with a sample before committing to a full run.

## What Makes a Great Wedding Invitation Cardstock?
Before diving into the top picks, it helps to understand what separates a flimsy sheet from a show-stopping invitation. The best wedding invitation cardstock types share three traits: weight, texture, and finish. Weight is measured in pounds (lb) for the US or grams per square meter (gsm); 110 lb (300 gsm) feels substantial without being bulky. Texture ranges from smooth to linen to cotton, each affecting how ink sits on the surface. Finish — matte, gloss, or shimmer — controls light reflection and sets the mood. A combination of these factors creates that "wow" moment when guests open the envelope.

## Top 7 Wedding Invitation Cardstock Types for 2025
Here are the seven cardstock types that will dominate 2025 wedding stationery, chosen for their versatility, printability, and aesthetic appeal.

### 1. Classic Matte Cardstock (110 lb / 300 gsm)
This is the workhorse of the invitation world. A smooth, uncoated matte finish absorbs ink beautifully, making it ideal for both digital printing and calligraphy. It's forgiving for beginners, works with any printer, and pairs perfectly with a [calligraphy font](https://www.creativefabrica.com/product/greenery-wedding-invitations-bundle/ref/8793785/?campaign=wedding2&utm_source=site2&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=2026-08-06) for a timeless look.

- **Weight**: 110 lb (300 gsm) — sturdy but easy to fold.
- **Texture**: Smooth, slightly toothy.
- **Best for**: Digital printing, laser printing, hand calligraphy.
- **Printing tip**: Use a high-quality inkjet with pigment-based ink to prevent smudging.

### 2. Cotton Paper (30% Cotton, 110 lb)
Cotton paper is the epitome of elegance. Its soft, fibrous texture gives invitations a luxurious, handmade feel that photographs like a dream. The surface is slightly absorbent, so ink dries quickly and colors appear muted but rich. It's a favorite among [The Knot](https://www.theknot.com) for luxury weddings.

- **Weight**: 110 lb (300 gsm) with a soft deckle edge option.
- **Texture**: Fibrous, with a subtle felt finish.
- **Best for**: Letterpress, foil stamping, calligraphy.
- **Printing tip**: Use a laser printer or a professional print shop; inkjet can bleed.

### 3. Pearl Shimmer Cardstock
If you want your invitations to catch the light, pearl shimmer is your answer. This cardstock has a subtle metallic sheen that shifts from white to soft iridescence, adding a touch of glamour without being over-the-top. It's perfect for evening weddings or any celebration with a champagne-and-gold palette.

- **Weight**: 110 lb (300 gsm).
- **Texture**: Smooth with a metallic micro-glitter embedded.
- **Best for**: Digital printing, foil accents, laser printing.
- **Printing tip**: Avoid heavy ink coverage; the shimmer can cause ink to pool.

### 4. Linen Textured Cardstock
Linen texture mimics woven fabric, adding depth and interest to your invitations. It's a classic choice for rustic, garden, or beach weddings. The texture is subtle enough for legible calligraphy but adds tactile appeal that guests will notice.

- **Weight**: 110 lb (300 gsm).
- **Texture**: Crosshatch linen pattern.
- **Best for**: Digital printing, letterpress, calligraphy.
- **Printing tip**: Use a matte finish to avoid glare; the texture can make fine details look slightly softer.

### 5. Vellum Overlay Cardstock (Not for Base)
While not a base cardstock, vellum overlays are a must-have for 2025. These translucent sheets layer over your invitation to create a dreamy, ethereal effect. Use them as a belly band, a wrap, or a full overlay with a printed pattern.

- **Weight**: 90 lb (170 gsm).
- **Texture**: Smooth, translucent.
- **Best for**: Overlays, belly bands, envelope liners.
- **Printing tip**: Print on vellum using a laser printer; inkjet will smear.

### 6. Kraft Cardstock (100 lb / 270 gsm)
For a rustic or eco-friendly wedding, kraft cardstock is a budget-friendly option with a natural, earthy look. It's made from recycled materials and has a warm brown hue that pairs beautifully with greenery and neutral palettes.

- **Weight**: 100 lb (270 gsm).
- **Texture**: Slightly rough, with visible fibers.
- **Best for**: Digital printing, rubber stamping, twine accents.
- **Printing tip**: Use a high-contrast ink color like white or deep green for readability.

### 7. Recycled Cotton Blend (50% Cotton, 110 lb)
Sustainability is a growing trend in 2025, and this blend offers the softness of cotton with a smaller environmental footprint. It has a slightly more textured surface than pure cotton but still prints beautifully and feels luxurious.

- **Weight**: 110 lb (300 gsm).
- **Texture**: Soft, with small flecks of recycled material.
- **Best for**: Digital printing, letterpress.
- **Printing tip**: Avoid fine serifs; the texture can make them look muddy.

## How to Choose the Right Cardstock for Your Printer
Your printer type is a critical factor. Inkjet printers work best with smooth, coated cardstocks (matte or pearl), while laser printers handle textured and cotton papers well. If you're using a home printer, always choose a 110 lb cardstock or thinner — anything heavier (like 130 lb) can jam. For professional results, consider using a local print shop that offers [Brides](https://www.brides.com) recommended services.

## How to Test Cardstock Before You Commit
Never order 200 sheets without testing. Here's a 3-step test:

1. **Print a sample**: Print your invitation design on 2-3 cardstock types using your printer settings.
2. **Check ink drying**: Wait 10 minutes, then gently rub a finger across the ink to see if it smudges.
3. **Test fold and mail**: Fold the sample and place it in an envelope — verify it fits without bulging.

## FAQ
### What is the best cardstock weight for wedding invitations?
The best weight is 110 lb (300 gsm). It feels substantial, prints well on most home printers, and fits standard envelopes without bulging. Heavier weights like 130 lb are reserved for luxury suites but require professional printing.

### Can I use regular paper for wedding invitations?
No. Regular 20 lb copy paper is too thin and flimsy; it will look cheap and may tear in the mail. Always use at least 80 lb cardstock, but 110 lb is recommended for a premium feel.

### How do I prevent ink smudging on textured cardstock?
Use a laser printer for textured papers like cotton or linen, as toner fuses to the surface. For inkjet, choose a matte cardstock and allow extra drying time (at least 15 minutes) before handling.

### What is the most elegant cardstock for calligraphy invitations?
Cotton paper (30% cotton) is the most elegant option. Its soft texture absorbs ink beautifully, giving calligraphy a velvety finish. Pair it with a fine-tip pen or a dip pen for best results.

### Are pearl shimmer cardstocks suitable for laser printers?
Yes, pearl shimmer cardstock works with laser printers, but the metallic coating can reflect heat unevenly. Use a lower heat setting and test a single sheet first to avoid bubbling.
