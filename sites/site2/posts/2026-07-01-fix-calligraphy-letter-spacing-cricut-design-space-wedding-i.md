It is the way Cricut Design Space handles letter spacing, or kerning, for script and calligraphy typefaces. Most built-in fonts and even many premium calligraphy fonts arrive in Design Space with default spacing that destroys the natural flow of handwriting.

## Why Cricut Design Space Destroys Calligraphy Kerning (And How to Outsmart It)

Cricut Design Space is a brilliant piece of software for cutting layered vinyl, paper, and cardstock. But it was not built for typography. The platform does not support OpenType features, which means it ignores the built-in kerning pairs and contextual alternates that make calligraphy fonts look natural.

### The Science Behind the Spacing

When a professional calligrapher writes a word by hand, each letter flows into the next. The tail of a lowercase ‘h’ curves to meet the loop of an ‘e,’ and the descender of a ‘g’ nestles under the preceding character. Font designers encode these relationships as kerning pairs. In Design Space, those pairs are stripped away, leaving each letter in its own isolated bounding box.

For example, take the word "Love." In a properly kerned calligraphy font, the ‘L’ and ‘o’ overlap slightly, creating a continuous stroke. In Design Space, the ‘L’ might sit a full millimeter away from the ‘o.’ On a 5x7 invitation printed on matte cardstock, this gap looks like a typo.

### What You Can Control

Design Space gives you two primary spacing controls: Letter Space (which adjusts spacing between all letters equally) and Line Space (for vertical spacing between lines of text). Neither of these solves the problem of uneven gaps between specific letter pairs. That is where manual adjustment comes in.

## The Critical Mistake That Ruins Calligraphy Spacing (And How to Avoid It)

Before we dive into the fix, you need to know the one mistake that will sabotage everything: applying a global Letter Space adjustment to a calligraphy font.

### Why Global Adjustment Fails

Setting Letter Space to -0.05 might close the gap between an ‘a’ and an ‘n,’ but it will also push an ‘r’ and an ‘i’ too close together, creating a muddy blob. Calligraphy fonts have wildly different spacing needs depending on the shape of each letter. A sweeping ‘W’ needs more room than a narrow ‘r.’ Global adjustments are designed for block serif and sans-serif fonts, not scripts.

### The Right Approach: Weld Individual Letters

The only way to achieve professional-level calligraphy spacing in Design Space is to weld letters together manually. This means adjusting the position of each letter relative to its neighbors, one pair at a time. It sounds tedious, and for a full invitation suite it does take time, but the result is worth it. Think of it as the difference between a fast-food burger and a handcrafted gourmet meal.

## Step-by-Step: How to Weld Calligraphy Fonts in Cricut Design Space

Here is the exact process I use for every wedding invitation I design. You will need your calligraphy font installed, your project open, and a bit of patience.

### Step 1: Type Your Text and Ungroup to Letters

Start by typing your text—for example, "Sarah & James." Do not adjust any spacing yet. Select the text box, then click "Advanced" in the top toolbar, and choose "Ungroup to Letters." This breaks your text into individual letter objects.

### Step 2: Adjust Each Letter Pair Manually

Zoom in to at least 150% so you can see the edges clearly. Click on the second letter of a pair (for example, the ‘a’ in ‘S-a-r-a-h’), and use the arrow keys on your keyboard to nudge it left or right. The fine movement of arrow keys is much more precise than dragging with a mouse.

**Pro tip:** Hold down the Shift key while pressing the left arrow to move the letter in smaller increments—this gives you pixel-perfect control.

### Step 3: Look for Overlap, Not Touch

Calligraphy letters should overlap slightly, not just touch. The stroke of one letter should visually continue into the next. For example, the exit stroke of an ‘S’ should sit just behind the entry stroke of the following ‘a.’ Aim for an overlap of about 0.5–1 millimeter on the screen.

### Step 4: Weld the Letters Together

Once you have all letters positioned correctly, select every single letter (Ctrl+A or Cmd+A), then click "Weld" in the bottom right corner of the Layers panel. This fuses them into one continuous shape, locking in your custom spacing.

### Step 5: Check Your Work with a Test Cut

Before cutting your final cardstock, do a test cut on scrap paper or vinyl. This is non-negotiable. What looks perfect on screen can appear different when cut. Adjust any pairs that look off in the physical cut, then re-weld.

## Three Methods for Fixing Calligraphy Spacing (From Quickest to Most Precise)

Depending on your time, skill level, and font choice, one of these methods may suit you better.

### Method 1: The Quick Weld (Best for Beginners)

Use this when you have a simple word (like "Love") and a font with fairly consistent stroke widths. Ungroup to letters, nudge each pair until they just overlap, then weld. You can finish a single word in 5–10 minutes.

### Method 2: The Group Weld (Best for Long Text)

For longer lines like "Mr. and Mrs. Johnson," the group weld saves time. Instead of adjusting every single pair, group all letters first. Then ungroup to letters, adjust only the problem pairs (like ‘J-o’ or ‘n-s’), and weld. This cuts the adjustment time by half.

### Method 3: The Manual Path Adjustment (Best for Perfect Results)

This is the expert method. After ungrouping to letters, convert each letter to a path by selecting it and clicking "Object" > "Path" > "Convert to Path." Then use the Path Edit tool to reshape the overlapping parts of each letter, creating a seamless connection. This takes practice but yields invitations that look entirely hand-drawn.

## Tools and Accessories That Make Spacing Easier

You do not need expensive equipment, but a few items will save you frustration.

### A Light Pad

A light pad (around $20 on Amazon) lets you see through your cardstock. Place your test cut over the design on-screen to compare spacing visually. This is especially helpful for checking overlapping letters.

### Precision Tweezers

After cutting, use tweezers to lift delicate calligraphy letters from the mat. Wide, flat-tipped tweezers prevent bending the thin strokes of script fonts. I recommend the Cricut Weeder tool set, but any craft tweezers work.

### A Digital Caliper

Measure the actual cut width of your letters. If your font cuts thinner than expected, you may need to increase the line thickness in Design Space before adjusting spacing. A $15 digital caliper from a hardware store is sufficient.

## Common Calligraphy Spacing Mistakes (And How to Fix Them)

Even after you master the weld method, a few pitfalls remain.

### Mistake 1: Not Adjusting for Font Size

Calligraphy fonts behave differently at 12pt versus 72pt. At small sizes, gaps look larger proportionally. Always adjust spacing at the final cut size, not at a preview size. If your invitation is 5x7 inches, type your text at that scale before ungrouping.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Swashes and Tails

Many calligraphy fonts include decorative swashes—long tails on ‘y,’ ‘g,’ and ‘f.’ These can overlap with the next line of text. After welding, check the vertical spacing between lines. Increase line spacing by 10–15% if swashes collide.

### Mistake 3: Forgetting to Mirror for HTV

If you are using heat transfer vinyl (HTV) for wedding favors or welcome bags, mirror your design before cutting. But do not mirror the spacing adjustment—mirroring flips the letters, but the weld remains intact. Just remember to mirror at the final step.

## How to Save Your Custom Spacing for Future Projects

Once you have spent 20 minutes perfecting the spacing for a particular font, you do not want to repeat the work. Here is how to preserve it.

### Create a Design Space Template

After welding, save the entire project as a template. You can then copy and paste the welded text into new projects. The welds stay intact. I keep a library of templates for the five calligraphy fonts I use most often.

### Export as SVG

For absolute flexibility, export your welded text as an SVG file. Right-click the welded shape, choose "Save as SVG," and store it in a folder. Next time, just upload the SVG into Design Space—no spacing adjustments needed. This is the fastest method for repeating designs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does my calligraphy font look spaced out in Cricut Design Space?

Cricut Design Space does not support OpenType kerning features. Calligraphy fonts rely on these features for natural letter connections, so they appear spaced out. You must manually adjust and weld letters to fix this.

### Can I use the Letter Space tool to fix calligraphy spacing?

No. The Letter Space tool applies a global adjustment to all letters equally, which does not work for calligraphy fonts. It will either leave gaps in some pairs or cause overlaps in others. Manual welding is the only reliable method.

### How do I know if my letters are overlapping enough?

Zoom in to 200% and look for a slight overlap—about 0.5–1.5 millimeters depending on the font. The exit stroke of one letter should visually connect to the entry stroke of the next. Perform a test cut to confirm.

### What is the best calligraphy font for beginners in Cricut Design Space?

Fonts with even stroke widths, like "Modern Calligraphy" or "Ruffle," are easier to adjust because they have fewer delicate swashes. Avoid high-contrast scripts like "Honey Script" until you are comfortable with welding.

### Can I reuse my welded text for other projects?

Yes. After welding, save the project as a template or export the welded shape as an SVG. You can then upload the SVG into any new project with no spacing adjustments needed.
